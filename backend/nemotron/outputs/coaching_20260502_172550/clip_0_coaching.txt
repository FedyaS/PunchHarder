# Guard & Stance  
Maintain a high, tight guard throughout. Your lead hand often drops below the chin during the jab (e.g., 3095‑3238 ms), exposing your face. Keep elbows tucked and shoulders relaxed to protect the head.

# Hip Rotation & Power  
The crosses (2188‑2649 ms, 6061‑6533 ms, 7070‑7332 ms) show solid hip drive, but the hooks (4629‑5007 ms, 9240‑9347 ms) lack full torso rotation. Rotate your hips and torso together to generate maximum power and keep the punch on a straight line.

# Balance & Footwork  
You’re shifting weight heavily onto the rear foot during the uppercuts (3521‑3976 ms, 5276‑5680 ms, 8029‑8079 ms). Work on staying more centered; a balanced stance lets you recover quickly and throw the next punch with speed.

# Rhythm & Timing  
The sequence is a bit erratic. The jab‑cross‑jab pattern (3095‑3238 ms, 7689‑7760 ms, 8378‑8602 ms) is good, but the timing between the uppercuts and the following jab (8029‑8079 ms → 8378‑8602 ms) is rushed. Aim for a smoother, more measured rhythm—pause briefly after each heavy punch before launching the next.

# Head Movement  
Your head stays relatively still, which is fine for a stationary shadowbox, but in a real fight you’ll need to slip and roll. Practice bobbing and weaving between combos to avoid counters.

# Summary  
Focus on keeping the guard up, rotating the hips fully on every punch, staying balanced on both feet, and establishing a consistent rhythm. These adjustments will make your combinations tighter and more effective in the next round.
